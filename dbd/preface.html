How this story works

Before You Begin

This is a book about dice, kind of.

You know those Choose-Your-Own-Adventure books? Where you-the-reader influence the path of the story using your ability to choose? This book is written by me-the-author and directed by dice rolls I’m obligated to make every time a character faces an interesting test. I have a general idea of where the plot is headed, but that can be upended at any time by cruel, fickle fate—also known as an unexpected 1 showing up on the topmost face of a 20-sided die.

Put another way, this is a book with rules that the writer has to follow. That means I need a set of rules to hold myself to—and ideally, a set of rules you recognize too. Fortunately, there’s a system built for that, provided courtesy of Wizards of the Coast, who generously offer a certain amount of their ruleset as open-source content anyone can use:

4. Grant and Consideration: In consideration for agreeing to use this License, the Contributors grant You a perpetual, worldwide, royalty-free, non-exclusive license with the exact terms of this License to Use, the Open Game Content.

Use is defined broadly, which is good news:

1(f). "Use", "Used" or "Using" means to use, Distribute, copy, edit, format, modify, translate and otherwise create Derivative Material of Open Game Content. 1(g). "You" or "Your" means the licensee in terms of this agreement.

That’s what gives me the right to use the rules, but it also comes with boundaries. I can’t use existing D&D characters, name-drop trademarked places or factions, or refer to spells with proper names in them (like TrademarkedCharacter’s Acid Arrow).

So I’m playing it safe. You’ll see classes and species you recognize, and doing something like rolling Initiative or making a Perception check will look just like it does at your table.

Here's how this book will work:

  • Dice rolls matter. Every time the MC does something that would usually call for a GM ruling, I’ll roll in a semi-public, verifiable way via Discord. That includes stat checks, Insight rolls, short rest HP recovery, persuasion attempts—you name it.
  • The world knows about the dice. Everyone in this setting is aware that their fate is ruled by the dice, which they usually perceive as a kind of stronger-than-luck force—something that respects skill, but isn't controlled by it. They understand leveling too, although in this story, it’ll happen on milestone-and-performance logic (think DanMachi).
  • Natural 20s fuel me. Every time the MC rolls a 20, I get to bank one “DM Favor”—essentially Advantage or Disadvantage I can apply to a roll of my choosing at a later moment. I can store up to three. This is my little cheat code to avoid the whole story TPKing at an anticlimactic moment.

That’s all you need to know. Hopefully, you’re now equipped to enjoy an adventure that starts with a level-0 commoner and (dice willing) ends with a level 20 master of the shadows.

If you’re someone who wants the full legal and structural info, hang tight. After the next break, I’ll include the full text of the OGL license—just in case.

Session 0

Just like a real table, nobody likes a weirdo DM-slash-author jumping over the boundaries of what their readers want, making everyone uncomfortable and ruining all the fun.

On the other hand, nobody likes a boring story. There needs to be some violence, and I want this to be a pretty realistic world where bad things happen.

The best way I can handle the balance between those two things is to give you an idea of what's coming via a set of rules I commit to.

This document might change a bit in the future as I add more restrictions, but you can rely on the following rules.

No Excessive Gore

There's going to be violence in this world. Things are going to bleed. Monsters will be stabbed.

What won't happen is torture or intentionally gross descriptions of injury, especially when it comes to things with human-like personalities.

Within this category, I'm also committing to something like "no focused violence on children". Kids aren't immortal, and something like a town burning down might come with casualties, but I'm not all that interested in showing that except in the vaguest ways.

No SA, No Harem, No Porn

Sort of self-explanatory, but this isn't a fetish novel. People can have all sorts of backgrounds and it's vaguely possible that there might be people with tragic past events at some point in the story. I'm not going to show that in real-time.

At the same time, this is not a work of erotica. Everyone is an adult and usual drama-as-mirror-of-life rules apply, but things like sex are going to be pretty lampshaded if they happen at all.

Only Slight Fantasy Species Tropes

Dwarves are tavern-guys who mine, and tend to be boisterous. Elves are tall and tend to be a little more proper. Humans are, you know, humans.

All that is true in this book, but only applied as truth with the lightest of touches. Something like a quiet, shy dwarf is possible here, even if they trend a certain way as a people. I'm not looking to write a boring book, overall, so while each species has a kind of bias, individuals might swim against that current.

Skin pigment differences exist, but I don't see people in a multi-sentient-humanoid world caring about that very much, so neither will I.
